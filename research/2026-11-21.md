   video on "income needed to afford a house" reached 3x in the last 90 days. Nearest: The Money Guy "Was Life
   Actually More Affordable in 1980?" 33.2x (a then-vs-now comparison, already counted for format #2); CNBC Make It

   Retirees Buy Homes Under $150,000"), but it is a 354-subscriber retiree channel that fails the entry bar. Press

   YourTango on @averagejoegam3's "$120K and this is the house we can afford", Jan 2026) but gives no views or
   baselines. Grade **C** for the topic (interest shown, no multiples). **Applies:** #4 stays "supported (format),
   topic needs proof". The failure is missing data, not a failed format.
2. **Better numbers for #4: Redfin publishes the income needed.** Redfin (Aug 5, 2026, June 2026 data, 30%-of-income
   rule, 15% down, mid-6% rates): **$109,796 a year** to afford the typical US home; median household income $87,599;
   only 34.2% of listings affordable to the median earner. Metros: San Francisco $453,205, Seattle $221,831, Austin
   $132,570, Pittsburgh $82,816, Detroit $66,999. Grade **A** (primary data, stated method).
   [Redfin](https://www.redfin.com/news/affordability-homebuying-2026/). CBS (May 2026) quotes an older $116,780; use
   Redfin. **Applies:** a cited source on screen instead of our own maths (the playbook bans equations on screen and
   prefers checked figures), and a "find your city" payoff that differs by viewer, which `runs/research.md` asks for.
   Our backlog maths uses 20% down and 7.28%; if Create uses Redfin, use Redfin's assumptions too and don't mix them.
3. **#16 family-of-4 spend: 1 of 3 accounts.** Shorts pull: Anthony O'Neal "$100 every time you leave the house, and

   [Fox Business](https://foxbusiness.com/media/working-moms-tearful-confession-family-living-paycheck-paycheck-multi-viral),

   **Applies:** it is exactly our audience's pain, from a near-zero account, so it goes in as an idea needing proof

   And it's Tuesday and you've got two hundred dollars until Friday."), or be Srini's own founder story, which is true.
5. **Platform guidance on openings: sound off and the first 3 seconds.** Adam Mosseri, in a Reel on reach tactics
