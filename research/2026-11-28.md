5. **Three new studies pitched (S35–S37 in `research/study-bank.md`, status pitched).** Srini, please mark each
   "send" or "no":
   - **S35** Two cards, two rates: about 85% of people should put all their extra payment on the higher-rate card;
     only about 10% did. Most split it in proportion to the balances (Gathergood, Mahoney, Stewart & Weber, *AER*
     2019; 1.4M UK cardholders, US replication). Grade **A** (observational). Move: check each card's APR, extra
     dollars to the highest rate.
   - **S36** People predict next week's spending too low because they picture a typical week; asking "why might
     this week be different?" made predictions more accurate (Howard, Hardisty, Sussman & Lukas, *JMR* 2022; 10
     studies, 6,044 people, one field study with a finance app). Grade **A** for the direction; the size of the gap
     comes from press coverage (£75 a week vs "$5,000+ a year"), so no number until it's pulled from the paper. Closest
     study yet to what safe-to-spend does.
   - **S37** Exceptional expenses: each "one-time" cost is judged as a one-off, so people underestimate and
     overspend on them; seeing them together reduced it (Sussman & Alter, *JCR* 2012). Grade **A** for the
     direction, no checked number. Move: one monthly "one-offs" line.
