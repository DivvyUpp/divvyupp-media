shorter Shorts break out more often, so the next Study Reels should be cut to 20–25 s?

**Bottleneck it serves:** watch time and sends. `metrics/2026-11-26.json` (Windsor, 05:55 ET): followers still 3;

## Findings
1. **Our Reels hold about the same 9–17 seconds whatever their length, so the end is seen by few.** Reels with 10+
   reached (`metrics/2026-11-26.json`, lengths from the files in `reels/`):

   | Reel | Length | Reached | Skip | Avg watch | Watch ÷ length |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | 2026-11-23-s2-aging (Srini on camera) | 32 s | 24 | 58.3% | 15.3 s | **0.48** |
   | 2026-11-20-same-10k-v12 | 53 s | 98 | 71.3% | 16.9 s | 0.32 |
   | 2026-11-21-dollar-bill-rules | 55 s | 30 | 80.6% | 14.8 s | 0.27 |
   | 2026-11-25-s7-gym v2 | 44 s | 12 | 91.7% | 9.0 s | 0.20 |
   | 2026-11-25-s8-minimum v2 | 54 s | 42 | 62.8% | 10.0 s | 0.18 |

   Grade **A for the numbers, but every Reel is under 100 views, so this is a pattern, not a verdict.** Applies:
   with Srini's opening order (hook → tip # and result → why → study → result → **fix at the end** → send prompt), a
   viewer who stays the average 10 s never reaches the fix or the send line. The send can't happen if the prompt is
   never seen. The shortest Reel, s2-aging, also had the best ratio and the lowest skip among these.

2. **Outside, length alone doesn't predict a breakout; the 30–60 s band, where our Study Reels sit, is the weakest.**
   698 Shorts from the 21 niche and brand channels (format teachers and SoFi left out), posted 3–90 days ago:

   | Length | Shorts | At 3x+ | Hit rate | Accounts with a 3x+ |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | under 20 s | 44 | 6 | 14% | 4 |
   | 20–30 s | 53 | 9 | 17% | 5 |
   | 30–45 s | 155 | 9 | **6%** | 4 |
   | 45–60 s | 142 | 13 | **9%** | 9 |
   | 60–90 s | 164 | 20 | 12% | 10 |
   | 90–180 s | 140 | 24 | 17% | 8 |

   The extremes win for different reasons. The under-30 s outliers (7 accounts) are mostly memes and quick hacks:
   George Kamel "Beans and rice it is!" 8 s, **15.1x** ([link](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/oVWKwgFeU1M)); NerdWallet
   "Finance Bros BIGGEST Money Fumbles" 27 s, 3.7x ([link](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/sh3K7GcQXH0)); Casper Capital
   brand hacks (barred). The 90 s+ outliers are talk-show couple calls and news: Rachel Cruze "Her Husband Said He'd Loan
   Her Money For Groceries" 105 s, 10.5x; Your Rich BFF news 90–150 s, up to 73.7x. **Within channels, outliers ran
   longer than that channel's other Shorts in 11 of 18**, so shorter is not a rule. Grade **B** (Shorts stand in for
   Reels; the API gives views, not watch time). Applies: no proof that short wins in general, but a 40–55 s Study Reel
   is in the band that breaks out least, and it neither delivers fast (like the short clips) nor carries a story with
   a person (like the long ones). New bet **#11**: cut one approved Study Reel to 20–25 s, same script order, and
   compare with its long cut in our own data.

3. **The ranking format keeps breaking out (bet #3).** Graham Stephan "Ranking The BEST Credit Cards" **75.8x** (Sep 23,
   [link](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/K7UvJQsjMOM)) is the biggest multiple in the pull; his "Ranking The BEST Money
   Books" is 14.8x. Grade **B**. Applies: adds weight to #3 (rules / ranking list). A Study Reel variant ("Ranking 5
   money habits by what the research says") could ride it later; not a bet today (no studies ranked yet).

4. **Audience words: people relate to cutting gift lists, and call it freeing.** Under George Kamel's "What Is Your
   Christmas Budget?" (1.5x, posted yesterday), four of the top comments say the same thing in different words: no
   gifts for adults, kids only ("We stopped buying gifts for adults years ago"; "This has been so freeing"). The two
   Ramsey gift Shorts are now **3.5x** (Rachel Cruze "Adults Don't Need Christmas Gifts") and **3.0x** (The Ramsey Show
   "Is She Cheap For Not Wanting To Give Everyone Gifts?"), but that's still one org. Grade **B**. Applies: three
   phrases added to `research/pain-language.md`; "gift obligation" stays a pain moment needing proof (1 org of 3).

5. **Four new studies pitched for Study Reels (S28–S31 in `research/study-bank.md`, status pitched).** Srini, please
   mark each "send" or "no":
   - **S28** On payday, people spend more, even those with plenty of cash in the bank (Olafsson & Pagel, *RFS* 2018,
     app data from Iceland). Grade **A** for the finding; the 70%/40% sizes come from the authors' slides, so check them
     before a Reel quotes a number. It's the closest study yet to DivvyUpp's "save first, the morning pay lands".
   - **S29** A credit-limit increase raised card debt right away, about 10–14 cents per extra dollar of limit, even for
     people far below their limit (Gross & Souleles, *QJE* 2002, US card data). Grade **A**. Move: turn down the
     automatic increase.
   - **S30** The tip buttons on the screen set the tip: higher buttons raised taxi tips by more than 10% at the cutoff,
     though more people then tipped nothing on the card (Haggag & Paci, *AEJ: Applied* 2014, 13 million NYC rides).
     Grade **A**. Move: decide your percentage before the screen turns around.
   - **S31** Offered a trial that renews by itself, about a quarter fewer people signed up, and many who stayed stopped
     reading (Miller, Sahni & Strulov-Shlain, working paper, 2.1 million readers of a European newspaper). Grade **A**
     (randomized), but versions differ (24% vs 35% fewer), so check the final paper. Move: set the cancel reminder the
     minute you sign up.
