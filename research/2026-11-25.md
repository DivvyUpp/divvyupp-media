3. **Gift-obligation comments relate instead of judging, unlike high-earner Shorts.** Under the Ramsey and Rachel Cruze
   gift Shorts, the top comments agree and add their own story ("forced generosity…", 460 likes, quoting the host;
   "Buying for 10+ people can get a bit costly…", 228 likes; "they just go into debt to show off", 76 likes). Under
   "Do You Remember What You Spent On Christmas?" people post their own totals ($300 to $7,000) and one wrote "Not sure

   nothing left" Shorts, top comments pile on. Grade **B** (like counts are data). **Applies:** five phrases added to
   `research/pain-language.md`. A money moment that viewers share with a family member invites "me too", which is
   the feeling behind a send.

4. **Three new studies pitched for Study Reels (S25–S27 in `research/study-bank.md`, status pitched).** All fit the
   week people shop most. Srini, please mark each "send" or "no":
   - **S25** A $25 cash-back reward raised card spending by about $79 a month and card debt by about $191 a month in
     the first quarter (Agarwal, Chakravorti & Lunn, Chicago Fed WP 2010-19, administrative data from one large US
     bank). Grade **A** (field data). Caveat: an earlier draft reports $68 and $115; some of the spending was shifted
     from other cards. Move: check what the reward cost you, not what it paid.
   - **S26** Getting access to buy now, pay later raised people's total spending by about $60 a week, mostly at
     retailers, and it didn't just move spending from later to now (Di Maggio, Katz & Williams, NBER w30508, 2022, US
     bank-account data). Grade **A** for the finding; the $60 figure is from one version of the paper (HBS PDF), so check
     the final version before quoting it. Move: before you split a purchase in four, ask if you'd buy it at the full
     price today.
   - **S27** Across 21 studies, lenient return policies (free, easy returns) raised purchases more than they raised
     returns (Janakiraman, Syrdal & Freling, *Journal of Retailing* 2016, meta-analysis). Grade **A** (meta-analysis),
     but there's no single headline number, so the hook has to rest on the direction. Move: decide as if you can't
     return it.
   Not pitched: Lewis, Singh & Fay (*Marketing Science* 2006) on free-shipping thresholds. Real study, but I could
   only see later papers' summaries, not its numbers.
