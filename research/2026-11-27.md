   In the wider search, 69 of 133 tier-list Shorts (on channels with a 1,000+ median) were 3x+, across 49 accounts.
   **That rate is inflated:** search by relevance surfaces Shorts that already did well, so read it as "the format
   breaks out often", not as a 52% hit rate. Length didn't hold them back: 39 of 79 tier lists over 60 s were 3x+.
   Grade **B** (Shorts as a proxy for Reels, views only). Applies: same shape as Study Reels (a trusted voice, a

   BrianDoesFinance "RANKING SAVINGS ACCOUNTS" 1.2x ([link](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/pLcwq4GTKew)), Yudi J credit cards 1.4x, Money Guy "Ranking Money Habits" 0.7x,
   Humphrey Yang "3 Tiers of Wealth" 1.0x, The Cold Ledger "Ranking Money Habits: S to F" 0.0x. (Many tiny money
   channels show 10–90x on medians of 10–100 views; I left those out as noise.) Grade **B**. Applies: 3 finance
   accounts at 3x+ makes the tier list proven for us too, but most money tier lists land around 1x, so the topic and
   the build decide it. The ones that broke out rank things the viewer owns or is choosing now (cards, where cash
   sits); "money habits" lists missed three times.

4. **Under a real one-income couple, the comments pile on one partner.** Caleb Hammer "Stay-At-Home Mom VS Dad With

   the wife ("That woman is clueless…", 6,292 likes; "Poor guy is TIRED!"). Only a few say both roles count. Grade

   examples, and the snark stays on the habit, never on one partner (we are one-income ourselves). Two phrases added to
   `research/pain-language.md`.

5. **Three new studies pitched (S32–S34 in `research/study-bank.md`, status pitched).** Srini, please mark each
   "send" or "no":
   - **S32** Put the thing in front of people and they'd pay about half again as much for it: bids for a real snack
     on a tray were 40–61% higher than for its photo or name; behind plexiglass the extra vanished (Bushong, King,
     Camerer & Rangel, *AER* 2010, Caltech lab). Grade **A**. Move: put it back and decide from the photo on your phone.
   - **S33** Showing a loan's cost in dollars over time, not as a rate, cut payday borrowing by about $55 per pay
     cycle, about 23% (Bertrand & Morse, *Journal of Finance* 2011, 1,441 borrowers, 77 stores). Grade **A**. One
     summary gives 11% on another measure, so check the paper before quoting. Move: write down what a balance costs
     in dollars by the time you'd pay it off.
   - **S34** Gift certificates with a three-week deadline got used more than ones good for two months, though the
     long-deadline holders expected to use theirs more (Shu & Gneezy, *JMR* 2010, field studies). Grade **A** for
     the direction. I couldn't see the percentages, so the Reel would give no number. Gift card season. Move: put the
     day you'll use the card in your calendar the day you get it.
   Not pitched: Kuchler & Pagel (JFE, people don't keep their own card paydown plans; no number I could quote) and
   Stango & Zinman (*JF* 2009, exponential growth bias; observational, no clear move).
