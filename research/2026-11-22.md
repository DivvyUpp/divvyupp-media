what kind of opening line and topic wrapper breaks out for money accounts, and which of our core topics hold up?

**Bottleneck it serves:** `metrics/2026-11-22.json` (Windsor, cached). Our best Reel, 2026-11-20-same-10k-v12

12:35pm ET) has 22 views, skip 80.0%, average watch 22.0 s, 0 shares. Followers are still 3 and non-follower views

## Findings
1. **Hooks that lead with a person in a money situation break out more than twice as often.** I matched titles that
   name a third person or a relationship (she, he, her husband, boyfriend, guest, mom…). Those titles were 3x+ outliers
   **22.6%** of the time (84 Shorts). All other titles hit 3x+ 9.5% of the time. Within each channel, these titles
   beat the channel's other titles in **7 of 9 channels** that had 3+ of each. The pattern is one person plus one
   specific money move, in 8 words or fewer:
   - NerdWallet "Wait… THAT Was Her Dealbreaker?!" 111,660 views vs median 2,026 = **55.1x** (Nov 17,
     [link](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/CHNkGgF2kks))
   - Ramsey: Rachel Cruze "Her Husband Said He'd Loan Her Money For Groceries" 433,346 vs 46,391 = 9.3x (Nov 16,
     [link](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/XKVmNot5ap8)); George Kamel "She's So Proud of Her $1,500 Car Payment"
     3.5x ([link](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/urpIlbvjIv4)); Ramsey Show "She Can't Afford Her Credit Card
     Payments" 6.7x ([link](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/WJrTuc7rYCo))
   - Ramit Sethi "We're Nearly $1M in Debt. But He Won't Change Jobs" 48,756 vs 9,924 = 4.9x (Oct 7,
     [link](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/qwQHt5EPUW8))
   - Mrs Dow Jones (7.0x), Caleb Hammer (4.8x), Graham Stephan (4.5x)

   Grade **B**. Caveat: most of these are real callers or interviewees, and ours would be drawn, clearly framed
   examples. That difference may matter. **Applies:** our hooks lead with "you" or with two totals. A hook like "She
   checked the account on the 23rd. $240. He says they're fine." is a single-variable test (new backlog **#5**, after the re-sort).
   *Note for Create:* in the pending couple Reel, hook A2 ("$11,000 a month between us…") is spoken in the first
   person. PLAYBOOK says a presenter is not a customer, so check that it reads as a framed example.
2. **Numbers in the title help within a channel, and "you" titles are a coin toss.** Titles with a $ or a digit beat
   the same channel's other titles in 13 of 17 channels. Pooled across channels they look worse (8.0% vs 12.3%), but
   that gap comes from which channels use numbers. Titles with "you"/"your" were up in 9 channels and down in 6, so
   there is no signal either way. Grade **B**. **Applies:** keep the specific figure in the hook (our persona
   reviewers already favour it). Putting the hook in "you" form does not help by itself. A person plus a number is
   the strongest combination in this pull.
3. **Our core topic words don't break out on their own, but the wrapper does.** In the last 90 days, no account had a
   3x+ Short with "paycheck" in the title (5 Shorts) or "month" (10 Shorts). "Budget" had 20 Shorts, and its only hit
   was a travel post (Casper Capital "3 Countries Paying You To Move" 4.6x). The Budget Mom's "Budgeting Never Works
   For Me" got 1.9x and she had 0 outliers across 44 Shorts. The same household-money pain does break out when it's
   wrapped in a person, a couple dilemma or a parent question. Examples: Rachel Cruze groceries 9.3x and "Thing I
   Will Not Do As A Mom" 4.5x ([link](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/tXAsswML9oc)); Kamel "4 Accounts Every Parent
   Should Know" 8.5x. Grade **B**. **Applies:** lead with the situation and the person, and keep the DivvyUpp
   mechanism (save first, daily number) for the turn. This is a third near-miss for #11 ("budget never lasts past
   week two"), and it kills #12 ("Signs you're…": Rachel Cruze "5 Signs You're Doing Better Than Most Americans" 1.3x,
   Humphrey "4 Signs…" 0.6x, the only two in the pull, both misses).
4. **Instagram counts seconds watched, not only the percentage.** Mosseri has said ranking looks at the share of a
   Reel watched and also the raw seconds watched. He has also said there is no ideal length: "it depends on how long
   it really takes to tell the story." I read this secondhand, through a search summary of
   [Lindsey Gamble](https://www.lindseygamble.com/blog/instagram-reveals-how-its-algorithm-weighs-watch-time-for-reels)
   and [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-longer-reels-hurt-performance/715867/).
   I could not open either page. Instagram also now shows a per-Reel **retention graph** in the app, according to
   [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-adds-retention-insights-reels/758464/), which
   I also read only as a summary. Vendor guides cite "25–35% left at the midpoint is average" with no source, so I
   graded that C and did not use it. Grade **B** for the seconds point, which is a platform statement relayed
   secondhand. **Applies:** v12's 16.6 s is the number to beat, alongside the 0.31 ratio. Together with yesterday's
   finding 3, this supports fixing the middle before cutting length. The retention graph would show the exact second
   viewers leave, and Windsor doesn't give us that (see proposed change 1).
5. **Money news framed as "what it means for your wallet" is proven again, but it fits us poorly.** Three orgs hit 3x+
   in the last 30 days:
   - Your Rich BFF "Congress is BLUE! What does that mean for YOUR wallet??" 1,512,225 vs 22,367 = **67.6x** (Nov 5,
     [link](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/L7WxBIyxxAQ)), plus 4 more news Shorts at 4.3–15.6x
   - Mrs Dow Jones "The Nvidia tea is wild" 22.0x (Nov 9, [link](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/qnwpJYMynTs))
   - Humphrey Yang "The Federal Reserve Just Raised Interest Rates For The First Time Since 2023" 3.9x (Sep 16,
     [link](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/2gOubOLfU8E))

   Grade **B**. 9 of the 13 news titles in the pull were 3x+. Most were about investing or politics, and they need a
   same-day turnaround. **Applies:** a household-money version (a rate move means your mortgage or card payment
   changes, so your safe-to-spend changes) is a new low-ease bet, **#6**. We set it aside on 2026-11-20 for speed.
   Our daily loop now makes that turnaround possible.
6. **New watchlist accounts, first read.** Casper Capital has 9 Shorts at 3x+ in 90 days, all "secret savings hack"
   or "truth behind" posts about named stores and brands (designer clothes 22.1x, Nike 19.6x, Amazon codes 10.2x,
   [link](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/jKH1G4Ekqzo)). Clark Howard does something similar ("Walmart+ & Sam's Club
   One membership?" 4.3x, travel 5.4x). That is 2 accounts, and the format depends on brand names, which our rules
   bar, so it is not a bet. VANNtastic has 4 mortgage-payoff Shorts at 3.0–3.9x (1 account), so mortgage stays an
   idea needing proof. Baddies & Budgets has 0 outliers in 37 Shorts (cash stuffing doesn't break out there). Grade
   **B**.
