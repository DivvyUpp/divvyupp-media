2. **A new pain moment passes: "feeling behind".** Three orgs at 3x+ in 90 days, each opening on the viewer's fear
   of being behind and answering with real comparison data:
   - Humphrey Yang "If you feel behind financially… here's some data on why." 328,953 vs median 80,836 = **4.1x**
     (Sep 9, [link](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/bFg4LzgiEgM))
   - George Kamel (Ramsey) "How Does Your Net Worth Stack Up Against a 35-Year-Old" 63,004 vs 12,316 = **5.1x**
     (Nov 5, [link](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/s-qm11T5zs8))
   - The Money Guy Show "Was Life Actually More Affordable in 1980?" 859,802 vs 22,186 = **38.8x**, 1,095 comments
     (Nov 8, [link](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/0WkHrQkPIWY))

   Grade **B**. It rides proven format #2 ("How do you compare?"), so it isn't independent proof of a new format, but
   it is the first pain moment with its own 3x+ evidence. In comments under these, people in the pain do speak up:
   "I make 500k and it doesnt feel like it at all", "doesn't feel that great with how bad everything is". **Applies:**
   new bet **#6**: open format #2 on the "feel behind?" moment, then the turn is that being behind is usually an
   order problem (savings last), not an income problem. Use medians, never means (finding 3).

3. **A big salary in the hook invites a pile-on, and an inflated-looking figure becomes the conversation.** Under
   Shorts about well-paid people with nothing left, the top comments mostly judge the person: "Being 193k in debt
   while making 380k is impressive" (226 likes, Ramsey 1.2x Short), "I don't want to hear about people who make 175k
   n whining about it!" (50 likes, Humphrey), "Make smarter spending decisions, this can be avoided" (101 likes) and
   "You're living above your means!" under a 40,906-view "makes over 6 figures and still living paycheck to paycheck"
   Short ([link](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/KrD6N0Vpu5A)). On the 38.8x Money Guy Short the top comment (4,811
   likes) disputes the income figure: "84k is not the average gen z salary", and several more call it a mean, not a
   median. The like counts are data; that they predict worse reach is my reading (**C**). **Applies:** our bio and
   several hooks say "you make good money". On camera, name the moment and a modest monthly figure (take-home, not
   salary) and let the viewer place themselves; never a six-figure salary in the first 6 s. New bet **#21** tests it.
   Any income figure on screen must be a median with its source. A divisive figure does drive comments, but
   backlash comments are not a signal we want.

4. **Where the audience's own words live, and what they sound like.** Commenters who are actually in the pain write
   short, plain, slightly ashamed lines, not "lifestyle creep": "it doesnt feel like it at all", "I have a broke
   mindset", "Learning to spend is much faster than learning to save though", "I feel guilty and still use money I
   earn … to pay for stuff like groceries". A dad's saying got 59 likes: a raise adding a zero to your paycheck
   doesn't mean adding one to everything you buy. Eight phrases are now in `research/pain-language.md` (two only
   secondhand, from search summaries of Reddit posts: "$400 left in checking before each payday" on $68K; "half my
   paycheck goes to daycare"). Grade **B** for the phrases as real usage, **C** that they will work as hooks.
   **Applies:** this is thin. YouTube comments under finance Shorts are dominated by onlookers; Reddit, where people
   describe their own situation, is the better source and is blocked here (below).

5. **The proven outliers this month are still person, relationship and comparison wrappers.** The fresh pull adds
   no new format to the proven list: Ramsey has 10 outliers at Kamel alone ("Beans and rice it is!" 15.7x, "She Has
   a Plan" 9.9x, "Frugal Hills I Will Die On" 7.3x, a rules list like bet #3), Graham Stephan's "Ranking…" series
   (credit cards 66.8x, books 13.2x, habits 8.2x), NerdWallet's dealbreaker now 58.6x. Grade **B**. **Applies:**
   bets #1–#5 hold; "Frugal Hills I Will Die On" 7.3x is a fresh rules-list outlier for #3.
