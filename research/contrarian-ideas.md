# Contrarian money truths for Send Reels

The bank of non-obvious claims that Send Reels are built on (PLAYBOOK "Send Reel format"). Srini judged none of these send-worthy on 2026-11-24 (see `research/study-bank.md`); they stay as verified
facts for context, not as Reel ideas. Create may only use rows
marked **verified**. Each one has a primary source and the exact claim it supports, worded no stronger than the
source allows. Research verifies candidates, adds new ones, and records rejected ones (runs/research.md).
Call-outs punch at the habit, never at a group of people or their income.

## Verified (2026-11-24)

| # | Call-out (viewer's words) | Contrarian truth (say it no stronger than this) | Source | Caveat to respect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| V1 | "I'll save whatever's left." | People who committed in advance to saving out of future raises went from saving 3.5% to 13.6% of pay in about 40 months. Deciding first beats saving the leftovers. | Thaler & Benartzi, "Save More Tomorrow", *Journal of Political Economy* 2004 ([summary](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/research/rustandy/social-impact-research/research-papers/2004/save-more-tomorrow-using-behavioral-economics-to-increase-employee-saving)) | It's one employer's plan; the DOL review rates its causal evidence low ([CLEAR](https://clear.dol.gov/study/save-more-tomorrow%E2%84%A2-using-behavioral-economics-increase-employee-saving-thaler-benartzi-2004)). Say "in one famous study", never "you'll quadruple your savings". |
| V2 | "It's just my card, it's fine." | In an MIT auction for sports tickets, people told to pay by credit card bid more than twice as much as people paying cash. | Prelec & Simester, "Always Leave Home Without It", *Marketing Letters* 2001 ([MIT Sloan](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/node/241)) | Small study with MIT students; say "in one MIT study", don't claim everyone overspends by 2x. |
| V3 | "If I just cut my lattes…" | Housing and transportation were about half of US household spending in 2024 (33.4% + 17.0%); food was 12.9%. The big bills matter more than the coffee. | US Bureau of Labor Statistics, Consumer Expenditures 2024 ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/opub/ted/2026/housing-and-transportation-accounted-for-50-percent-of-household-spending-in-2024.htm)) | Averages across all households; don't call small spending irrelevant, just smaller. |
| V4 | "It's only 4 payments of $25." | The CFPB found Buy Now, Pay Later borrowers were much more likely than non-users to be highly indebted and to overdraft. | CFPB, *Consumer Use of Buy Now, Pay Later*, March 2023 ([CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/research-reports/consumer-use-of-buy-now-pay-later-insights-from-the-cfpb-making-ends-meet-survey/)) | Correlation, not cause: many differences predate BNPL use. Never say "BNPL causes debt". |

## Candidates (need verification before use)
- Tapping to pay (contactless) makes people less aware of what they spend. Lead: research on contactless cards and
  spending awareness; find the primary paper and its sample.
- "Your balance is lying": pending and upcoming bills aren't in the number you check. Our own product logic; needs a
  neutral source on how many people check balance before spending (or keep it as plain math, no statistic).
- People underestimate what they spend on subscriptions. Find a survey with a disclosed method.
- Lifestyle creep: raises tend to raise spending, not saving. Find a primary study.
- "Retail therapy" after a bad day. Find research on emotional spending, not opinion pieces.
