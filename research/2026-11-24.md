## Findings
1. **Outliers on evidence-based channels open on a belief the evidence corrects, not on the study.** Of 177 Shorts
   from Renaissance Periodization, Peter Attia, Andrew Huberman, Ali Abdaal, Doctor Mike, The Diary Of A CEO and
   Jeff Nippard, only 5 titles even mention a study, research or data. The 3x+ outliers frame the expert overturning
   something people believe:
   - Peter Attia "Why Peter changed his mind on Zone 2 training" 299,400 vs median 21,829 = **13.7x** (Oct 25,
     [link](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/igeLOCTR1MM))
   - Renaissance Periodization "Exercise Scientist Debunks Lifting Myths" 651,370 vs 52,319 = **12.4x** (Nov 11,
     [link](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/yLxMc0nTce4)); "The Huge Problem with Functional Training" **5.9x** (Nov 10,
     [link](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/uESsb2Ygloo))
   - Doctor Mike "Fake it till you make it?" 10.3M vs 2.47M = **4.2x** (Sep 22,
     [link](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/HDGWIGjlWhM))
   - In our own niche: The Money Guy Show "Was Life Actually More Affordable in 1980?" 876,592 vs 22,013 = **39.8x**
     (Nov 8, [link](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/0WkHrQkPIWY)), data against a common belief.

   Across the 7 channels, belief-correcting titles (myth, debunk, problem with, changed his mind, actually…) were 3x+
   outliers 19% of the time (26 Shorts) vs 11% for the rest (136). **Counter-evidence:** in our 21 finance channels
   (90 days), titles with "truth/myth/wrong/actually" hit 3x+ only 5% of the time (77) vs 10% for the rest (667);
   most of those are brand "truth behind" and news posts, a different thing. Grade **B** (titles stand in for spoken
   hooks; Shorts stand in for Reels). **Applies:** 4 unrelated accounts at 3x+ in 90 days meets the proof bar for the
   *hook form*. New bet **#7**: open a Study Reel on the belief the study overturns, in the viewer's words ("'Just
   remember to save.'"), then the study as the correction. It tests against PLAYBOOK's current Study Reel hook
   (move + result). Study-titled Shorts themselves don't break out: the study is the proof, not the hook.

2. **"The expert changed his mind" is a strong single example, not yet a format.** Attia 13.7x is the biggest
   outlier in the pass; Renaissance Periodization "Why Dr Mike Doesn't Lift As Heavy Anymore" is 3.1x
   ([link](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/k2cXfFTCB-4)) and "Jeff Nippard Cut His Workouts in HALF" 2.8x, just under
   the bar. That's 2 accounts. Grade **B**. **Applies:** Srini on camera saying "I used to think ___, then I read
   this study" would fit his founder story; it goes in as needs-proof bet **#22**.

3. **What doesn't break out here: protocol lists and tips.** Huberman's outliers are topic titles ("ENTRAINMENT
   GOVERNS YOUR ENERGY & FOCUS" 6.6x); Ali Abdaal's are money and books ("How much money do you need to feel
   financially free?" 5.8x, [link](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/_HI_QPwLlGw)); Jeff Nippard has 0 outliers in 120
   days on a 6.6M median; The Diary Of A CEO's are guest drama. No consistent "here's what the study says, do this"
   title among the outliers. Grade **B**. **Applies:** keep the move in the Study Reel (Srini's send test needs it),
   but don't lead with it in every Reel; bet #7 tells us which opening wins.

4. **Four new studies pitched for Study Reels (S21–S24 in `research/study-bank.md`, status pitched).** Srini, please
   mark each "send" or "no":
   - **S21** $5 a day vs $150 a month: about 4x the sign-ups for the daily frame, same money; it also closed the
     income gap (Hershfield, Shu & Benartzi, *Marketing Science* 2020, ~9,000 app users; one author is on the app's
     board). Grade **A** (field experiment). It is also the closest study yet to DivvyUpp's daily number.
   - **S22** Card payment reminders cut late fees 14% and raised overdraft fees 9% (Medina, *RFS* 2021, randomized,
     Brazil). Grade **A**. The move (check what's still due before you pay) is exactly safe-to-spend.
   - **S23** Card bidders offered roughly twice as much for the same Celtics tickets (Prelec & Simester 2001, MIT MBA
     students). Grade **A** for the study, but exact figures come from secondary sources; check the paper before quoting.
   - **S24** Just answering survey questions about overdraft fees cut overdrafts for up to two years (Stango & Zinman,
     *RFS* 2014). Grade **A** for the finding; no effect size in the abstract yet.
   Not pitched: Bertrand & Morse 2011 (dollar-cost disclosure cut payday borrowing by 23% per pay cycle, J-PAL):
   solid, but payday loans aren't our audience's pain.
