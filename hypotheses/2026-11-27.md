**2026-11-27-s13-card**
0. [curious] Think paying with debit keeps your grocery spending in check?
1. Here's research-backed money tip number eight. In a study of real grocery receipts, one thing at the register changed how much junk food ended up in the cart.
2. Here's why. Handing over cash feels like paying. Tapping a card, credit or debit, barely feels like anything.
3. Researchers looked at six months of grocery receipts from a thousand households.
4. Baskets paid by card had more impulse junk food: chips, candy, cookies. And debit was no better than credit.
5. So make the money feel real again. Before your next grocery run, write a list and set an amount.
6. [warm] Send this to whoever does the grocery run on debit.

**2026-11-27-s14-five**
0. [curious] Feel guilty spending even five dollars on anything that isn't a bill?
1. Here's research-backed money tip number nine. In a study published in Science, five dollars spent one way made people happier than twenty dollars spent the other way.
2. Researchers gave students five or twenty dollars in the morning, to spend by evening, either on themselves or on someone else.
3. That evening, the students who spent it on someone else were happier. And the amount made no difference. Five dollars worked as well as twenty.
4. So if five dollars feels hard to spend on you, spend it on someone else this week. A coffee, a snack, anything. Then notice how you feel.
5. [warm] Send this to the person you'd buy that coffee for.
