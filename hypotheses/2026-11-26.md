**2026-11-26-s10-joint**
0. [curious] Half the bills on your card, half on theirs, and nobody knows what's left?
1. Here's a research-backed money tip. In a two-year experiment, couples who made one change stayed happier. The others got less happy.
2. Researchers gave two hundred thirty engaged or newly married couples a joint account, separate accounts, or their own choice, at random.
3. After two years, the joint-account couples had kept their relationship quality, and fought less about money. The others showed the usual decline. One caveat: about one in five couples dropped out.
4. You don't have to merge everything. Start with one shared account for shared costs, and keep your own too.
5. [warm] Send this to your partner, and set it up together.

**2026-11-26-s11-fights**
0. [curious] Does every money talk in your house start with "what's this charge?"
1. Here's a research-backed money tip. Of everything couples argue about, one topic predicted divorce best. And it's not chores.
2. Researchers followed over four thousand five hundred US couples over time, and compared what they disagreed about: chores, time together, money.
3. Money disagreements predicted divorce most strongly. It's survey data, so it's a link, not proof of cause.
4. So don't wait for the next surprise charge. Do a fifteen-minute money check-in every week.
5. [warm] Send this to your partner with one question: Sunday, fifteen minutes?

**2026-11-26-s8-short**
0. [curious] Are you only making the minimum payment on your credit card?
1. Research-backed money tip. One change almost doubled what people paid toward their card.
2. The minimum on your statement pulls your payment down toward it.
3. In a UK lab study, people who never saw a minimum paid about a hundred seventy-five pounds, versus ninety-nine.
4. So set your autopay to a fixed amount you choose. Never minimum.
5. [warm] Send this to anyone with autopay on minimum.
